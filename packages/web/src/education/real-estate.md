# Real Estate

Real estate can be a useful part of a diversified portfolio, but it behaves differently from stocks and bonds.

## Ways to Invest in Real Estate

| Approach | What It Is | Liquidity |
|----------|------------|-----------|
| Primary residence | The home you live in | Low |
| Rental property | Direct ownership of income-producing property | Low |
| REITs | Publicly traded real estate investment trusts | High |
| Real estate crowdfunding | Private pooled real estate investments | Very low |

## Returns Come From Two Sources

1. **Price appreciation** — the property or fund becomes worth more over time.
2. **Income** — rent payments or REIT dividends.

Historically, rental income has made up a large portion of total real estate returns. A price-only index like Case-Shiller does not capture rental income.

## Pros and Cons

**Pros:**
- Potential inflation hedge
- Steadier prices than stocks in many periods
- Rental income can be predictable
- Leverage can magnify returns

**Cons:**
- Illiquid and expensive to sell
- Concentrated in one location or property type
- Ongoing maintenance, taxes, and insurance costs
- Leverage can also magnify losses

## A Balanced View

Real estate has historically returned less than the broad stock market but with lower volatility. Most long-term investors benefit from some real estate exposure, often through low-cost REIT index funds.
