# Reading Charts & Candlesticks

A stock chart is simply a visual record of what buyers and sellers have agreed a company is worth at different points in time. Learning to read it is the first step toward making informed decisions instead of guessing.

## Why Charts Matter

Charts let you see:

- **Trend direction** — is the stock generally going up, down, or sideways?
- **Volatility** — how much does the price swing day to day?
- **Support and resistance** — where have buyers or sellers repeatedly stepped in?
- **Volume** — how much conviction is behind a move?
- **Entry and exit timing** — where might risk be smallest relative to reward?

No chart tells the future. It only shows what has happened and where the crowd has historically changed its mind.

## Timeframes

Before reading a candle, check the timeframe. A single candle can represent:

| Timeframe | What one candle shows |
|-----------|----------------------|
| 1 minute | One minute of trading |
| 15 minutes | One 15-minute period |
| 1 hour | One hour |
| 1 day | One full trading day |
| 1 week | One week |
| 1 month | One month |

A pattern on a 1-minute chart is far less significant than the same pattern on a daily or weekly chart. Longer timeframes filter out noise.

## Anatomy of a Candlestick

A candlestick shows four prices from a single period:

- **Open** — the first traded price.
- **High** — the highest traded price.
- **Low** — the lowest traded price.
- **Close** — the last traded price.

### Body and Wicks

- **The body** is the rectangle between the open and close.
- **The wicks** (or shadows) are the thin lines above and below the body, showing the high and low.

A **green** (or hollow) candle means the close was above the open. Buyers controlled the period.

A **red** (or filled) candle means the close was below the open. Sellers controlled the period.

### What the Shape Tells You

| Candle Shape | Interpretation |
|--------------|----------------|
| Long green body | Strong buying pressure |
| Long red body | Strong selling pressure |
| Small body | Indecision between buyers and sellers |
| Long upper wick | Sellers rejected higher prices |
| Long lower wick | Buyers rejected lower prices |
| No wicks | One-sided control from open to close |

## Common Single-Candle Signals

These are not guarantees, but they are places where the balance of power may be shifting.

### Doji

The open and close are nearly the same. The body is a thin horizontal line. It signals indecision, especially after a strong trend.

### Hammer

A small body near the top of the candle with a long lower wick. It appears after a decline and suggests buyers stepped in to reverse the drop.

### Shooting Star

A small body near the bottom of the candle with a long upper wick. It appears after a rise and suggests sellers rejected the advance.

### Engulfing Patterns

- **Bullish engulfing:** a green candle whose body completely covers the previous red candle. Buyers have taken control.
- **Bearish engulfing:** a red candle whose body completely covers the previous green candle. Sellers have taken control.

## Reading Volume

Volume is the number of shares traded. It confirms or questions the strength of a price move.

- **Rising price + rising volume** = healthy uptrend.
- **Rising price + falling volume** = weak trend, may reverse.
- **Falling price + rising volume** = strong selling pressure.
- **Falling price + falling volume** = selling may be exhausted.

A breakout above resistance on heavy volume is more trustworthy than one on light volume.

## Support and Resistance

- **Support** is a price level where buying has historically been strong enough to stop declines.
- **Resistance** is a price level where selling has historically been strong enough to stop rallies.

These levels are not magical lines. They are zones where many traders remember making decisions, so they tend to act again when price returns.

When support breaks, it often becomes resistance. When resistance breaks, it often becomes support.

## Trends and Moving Averages

- **Uptrend:** a series of higher highs and higher lows.
- **Downtrend:** a series of lower highs and lower lows.
- **Sideways trend:** price oscillates between support and resistance without a clear direction.

A moving average smooths price action and helps you see the trend. Common periods are 20, 50, and 200 days.

- Price above a rising moving average suggests an uptrend.
- Price below a falling moving average suggests a downtrend.
- A moving average that has been support and then breaks can signal a trend change.

## Putting It Together

A good chart reading habit is to ask:

1. What is the overall trend?
2. Where are support and resistance?
3. What is volume telling me about this move?
4. What do the most recent candles suggest about buyer/seller control?
5. Is there a clear place to set a stop loss if I am wrong?

Charts are a language of probability, not certainty. The goal is to find setups where the odds are in your favor and the risk is clearly defined.
