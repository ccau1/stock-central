# Stock Allocation

Once you decide what share of your portfolio goes into stocks, the next question is how to divide that stock portion across different styles and risk levels.

## Conservative / Beginner Template

This mix emphasizes diversification and stability while still offering growth:

| Category | Allocation | Description |
|----------|------------|-------------|
| Broad-market ETFs | 40% | Total-market or S&P 500 index funds |
| Blue-chip stocks | 30% | Large, established, dividend-paying companies |
| Growth stocks | 20% | Companies expected to grow faster than the market |
| Risky equities | 10% | Small caps, speculative picks, or individual bets |

## More Ambitious Template

Investors with higher risk tolerance and a longer time horizon can shift toward growth:

| Category | Allocation | Description |
|----------|------------|-------------|
| Broad-market ETFs | 25% | Core market exposure |
| Blue-chip stocks | 25% | Stability and dividends |
| Growth stocks | 35% | Higher-growth companies |
| Risky equities | 15% | Speculative positions — the recommended ceiling |

## Using Margin

Some experienced investors use margin to amplify returns. It also amplifies losses.

- Avoid margin until you have years of experience.
- Never use more than half your available margin unless you are highly experienced.
- A margin call can force you to sell at the worst possible time.
- Losses can exceed your initial investment.

For most investors, the safer path is to avoid margin and let compounding do the work.
