# Asset Allocation

Asset allocation is how you divide your investments among different asset classes. It is one of the most important decisions an investor makes.

## Main Asset Classes

| Asset Class | Typical Role | Risk Level |
|-------------|--------------|------------|
| Cash & equivalents | Stability and liquidity | Low |
| Bonds / fixed income | Steady income, lower volatility | Low to moderate |
| Stocks / equities | Long-term growth | Moderate to high |
| Real estate | Income, diversification, inflation hedge | Moderate |
| Commodities | Inflation hedge, diversification | High |

## Age-Based Starting Point

A common rule of thumb is to hold your age in bonds and the rest in stocks. For example, a 30-year-old might start with 30% bonds and 70% stocks.

This is only a starting point. Your actual allocation should reflect:

- Your time horizon
- Your ability to handle volatility
- Your job stability
- Your other assets or liabilities

## Rebalancing

Over time, markets move and your allocation drifts. A portfolio that started at 70/30 stocks/bonds might become 80/20 after a strong stock market run.

Rebalancing means selling some of the winners and buying more of the laggards to return to your target allocation. Many investors rebalance once or twice a year, or when any asset class drifts more than 5–10% from target.
