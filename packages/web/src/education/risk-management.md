# Risk Management

Investing is about taking calculated risks, not avoiding risk entirely. Good risk management means understanding what can go wrong and limiting exposure to losses you cannot recover from.

## Types of Risk

| Risk | What It Means | How to Manage It |
|------|---------------|------------------|
| Market risk | The whole market falls | Long time horizon, diversification |
| Concentration risk | Too much in one stock or sector | Position limits, index funds |
| Liquidity risk | You cannot sell when needed | Emergency fund, liquid holdings |
| Leverage risk | Borrowed money amplifies losses | Avoid or strictly limit margin |
| Inflation risk | Purchasing power erodes | Own growth assets like stocks and real estate |

## Position Sizing Rules

- No single stock should make up more than 5–10% of your portfolio unless you are willing to lose that amount.
- Speculative or risky equities should stay under 15% of your stock allocation.
- If a position doubles, consider trimming it back to your target size.

## Behavioral Risk

The biggest risk for many investors is their own behavior:

- Panic selling during downturns
- Chasing hot stocks after they have already risen
- Checking portfolios too often and overreacting
- Abandoning a plan during volatility

A written investment plan helps you stay disciplined when emotions run high.

## Know Your Personal Risk Tolerance

Ask yourself: "If my portfolio dropped 30% tomorrow, would I sell, hold, or buy more?" Your honest answer should shape your allocation.
