# Budgeting & Expenses

A budget is not a restriction — it is a plan for your money. Every dollar that comes in should have a job before the month begins.

## The 50/30/20 Framework

A simple starting point for after-tax income:

| Category | Share | What It Covers |
|----------|-------|----------------|
| Needs | 50% | Housing, utilities, transportation, groceries, minimum debt payments |
| Wants | 30% | Dining out, entertainment, hobbies, subscriptions |
| Savings & debt | 20% | Emergency fund, investments, extra debt payments |

These percentages are a guideline, not a law. In high-cost areas, housing alone may exceed 30%, so adjust the other categories accordingly.

## Track, Then Trim

Before you can improve your budget, you need to know where money actually goes. Most people are surprised by how much they spend on:

- Subscriptions and memberships
- Dining out and delivery
- Impulse online purchases
- Unused services or apps

Review the last three months of transactions and group them into categories. Once you see the numbers, decisions become easier.

## Align Spending With Goals

A budget works best when it is tied to a goal:

- **Short term** — build a $1,000 mini emergency fund, pay off a credit card.
- **Medium term** — save for a car, a move, or a wedding.
- **Long term** — invest for retirement or financial independence.

Every spending decision is a trade-off with those goals.
